How gold settles, and the controls

Gold is over nineteen times heavier than water. Floods carry it down the stream, and it drops out wherever the current slows: in the slack water behind boulders, on the inside of bends and in the pool below the fall. Look closely at the bed in calm water and you may catch it glinting.

Swirl the pan under the water so the heaviest grains sink. Tilt the lip away from you and let the current wash the top layer off. Swirl again, wash again, until only black sand is left. Tilt too steeply and gold goes over the lip with the gravel.

Touch: the stick moves the ring. It sits at the bottom centre of the controls, and at the bottom centre of the stream in Full view. Push it to the edge to go faster, drag the stream to look around, pinch to zoom, and tap the riverbed to dig right there. Keyboard: W, A, S and D or the arrow keys move the ring, Shift goes faster, Q and E turn, R and F tilt the view, plus and minus zoom, Enter digs. Mouse: drag to look, scroll to zoom, click the riverbed to dig. In the pan, draw circles or hold Space to swirl, drag up or hold W to tilt and wash, and press Esc to tip it out. The strip above the controls jumps you along the stream.

Every stream is built fresh: beaches, bays, creeks that fork off into the woods, and an island with its own side channel. Each place is marked on the strip and turns gold once you reach it. Beaches, bays and creek mouths are where the water slows, so they are worth a pan.

The ring is also a detector. Over workable gravel it glows from cold blue through green and gold to hot orange as the gold nearby gets richer, and with sound on it beeps faster. Somewhere in every stream lies one pay streak, the richest run of gravel in it; the detector is the only way to find it.

When you turn the view, the camera stays over the water or just past its edge, so it never swings through the trees.

Tap Gold rush for the trail of claims. Each claim is one stream with one goal and a few pans; finish with pans to spare for more stars. Quests and chests pay out boosters and gold. All of it is simulated, and nothing in the game is for sale.

The water's light, from the moving wave surface to its sun glints, traced caustics and lens glare, is adapted from Clearwater by Aurélien, used under the MIT License. The ripples on the pools are calibrated from 60 fps footage of real water in a light breeze: their size, how long their crests run, which way they travel and how fast.
